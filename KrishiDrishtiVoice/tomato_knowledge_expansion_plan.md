# Tomato Knowledge-Base Expansion Plan

Research and planning only — prepared 2026-11-10

## Scope and constraints

This proposal expands only the tomato domain for Karnataka-focused use. It does not change `knowledge.json`, `agriculture_engine.py`, `app.py`, `gnani_service.py`, `.env`, or dependencies.

The existing `knowledge.json` uses a versioned object with `scope`, `reviewed_on`, and an `entries` array. Each entry currently contains `id`, `crop`, `category`, `topic`, `keywords`, English/Kannada descriptions, possible causes, distinguishing signs, follow-up questions, safe actions, referral conditions, region and growth-stage applicability, a source object, review date, evidence status, and limitations.

The expansion should preserve that structure. Every new record must remain explicitly triage-oriented: an observed symptom is not a confirmed disease, and no pesticide dose or fertilizer quantity should be generated by the local engine.

Evidence labels mean:

- **Strong**: the inspected source directly supports the narrow claim proposed for the record.
- **Partial**: the source supports the topic or broad association, but not enough detail for symptom-level diagnosis or a local treatment recommendation.
- **Insufficient**: the inspected sources do not support a safe record yet; keep the topic in the research backlog.

## Proposed record guidance

The following is a planning specification, not yet farmer-facing content.

### Diseases

#### `tomato-early-blight-001` — partial evidence

- Observable symptoms to research: leaf spots, possible concentric-ring descriptions, lower-leaf progression, and defoliation only after a source that states these details is inspected.
- Confusions: other foliar spots, nutrient stress, late blight, and physical injury.
- Required questions: Are spots circular or irregular? Do they have rings? Which leaves are affected? Is there rapid spread after wet weather? Are stems or fruit affected?
- Safe preliminary advice: photograph several leaves, record crop age and recent wet weather, and avoid naming the disease from a single image or symptom.
- Refer when: spotting is spreading, plants are defoliating, or treatment is being considered.

#### `tomato-late-blight-001` — partial evidence

- Observable symptoms to research: water-soaked or rapidly expanding lesions and fruit/stem involvement only from a source that explicitly describes them.
- Confusions: early blight, bacterial leaf spot, sun injury, and other moisture-associated damage.
- Required questions: How fast did symptoms appear? Were there recent rains or cool humid conditions? Are lesions on leaves, stems, or fruit?
- Safe preliminary advice: document the pattern and avoid moving visibly affected plant material between plots.
- Refer when: symptoms progress rapidly or whole plants/fruit are threatened.

#### `tomato-bacterial-wilt-001` — partial evidence

- Observable symptoms to research: rapid wilt, plant death, and stem or vascular observations only where the source directly supports them.
- Confusions: Fusarium wilt, water stress, root damage, and waterlogging.
- Required questions: Is wilting rapid? Does the plant recover overnight? Is the soil wet? Are multiple plants affected in patches? Has a qualified expert examined a stem?
- Safe preliminary advice: isolate observations, avoid redistributing soil or plant debris, and seek plant-health assessment.
- Refer when: rapid wilt or patch spread is present; do not recommend a chemical treatment from this record.

#### `tomato-fusarium-wilt-001` — insufficient evidence

- The inspected authoritative pages did not provide enough directly verifiable tomato-specific symptom detail for a safe record.
- Keep as a research item. Do not expose it as a likely diagnosis.

#### `tomato-leaf-curl-virus-001` — partial evidence

- Observable symptoms to research: leaf curling, stunting, and association with whitefly/vector observations only after inspecting a direct plant-pathology source.
- Confusions: herbicide injury, thrips or mite damage, heat stress, and nutrient imbalance.
- Required questions: Are young leaves affected? Is growth stunted? Are whiteflies visible? Is the pattern clustered? What is the crop stage?
- Safe preliminary advice: record the affected plant parts and consult a plant-health service before removing or treating plants.
- Refer when: symptoms are widespread, young plants are affected, or a virus is suspected.

### Nutrient deficiencies

#### `tomato-nitrogen-deficiency-001` — insufficient evidence

- Yellowing and stunting can be confused with water stress, root problems, disease, and natural ageing.
- Required questions: Which leaves changed first? Is growth stunted? What is the soil test? What was applied recently? Is the problem uniform across the plot?
- Safe preliminary advice: do not recommend a fertilizer quantity without crop stage, soil test, cultivar, irrigation context, and a current local recommendation.
- Refer when: symptoms are widespread or a fertilizer correction is being considered.

#### `tomato-magnesium-001` — insufficient evidence

- Interveinal chlorosis can be confused with iron or other nutrient problems and disease.
- Required questions: Are older or younger leaves affected? Are veins staying green? Is the pattern uniform? Is a soil or tissue test available?
- Safe preliminary advice: collect observations and seek soil/plant testing; do not infer a deficiency from color alone.
- Refer when: supplementation or fertigation is being considered.

### Irrigation and environmental stress

#### `tomato-waterlogging-001` — strong evidence

- Supported planning claim: the inspected IIHR crop-production page says well-drained soil is preferred and that waterlogging increases bacterial-wilt incidence.
- Confusions: bacterial wilt, root disease, drought after root damage, and heavy-rain foliar/fruit problems.
- Required questions: How long does water stand? Is the field on raised beds? Are symptoms patchy? What was the recent rainfall/irrigation?
- Safe preliminary advice: observe drainage and avoid changing chemical or fertilizer inputs based only on yellowing or wilt.
- Refer when: standing water persists, plants wilt rapidly, or disease is suspected.

#### `tomato-water-stress-001` — partial evidence

- Supported planning claim: IIHR describes stage-based drip irrigation and daily irrigation decisions based on crop factor and evaporation; this supports asking about irrigation context, not prescribing a universal schedule.
- Confusions: root disease, waterlogging, heat stress, and nutrient stress.
- Required questions: What is the crop stage? What irrigation method is used? When was the last irrigation/rain? What is the soil texture and moisture at root depth?
- Safe preliminary advice: check soil moisture and drainage before adjusting irrigation.
- Refer when: wilting persists despite adequate moisture or the field shows uneven stress.

#### `tomato-heat-stress-001` — partial evidence

- Supported planning claim: inspected ICAR-IIVR and IIHR pages describe tomato as a warm-season crop and discuss high-temperature-tolerant varieties; they do not provide enough Karnataka-specific symptom guidance for this record.
- Confusions: water stress, viral symptoms, blossom-end disorders, and sun injury.
- Required questions: What were the recent day/night temperatures? Which plant parts are affected? Is irrigation adequate? Is the crop protected or open field?
- Safe preliminary advice: record weather and irrigation conditions; avoid claiming heat stress without environmental evidence.
- Refer when: flowering/fruiting is severely affected or symptoms persist after weather changes.

### Pest-related symptoms

#### `tomato-whitefly-001` — partial evidence

- Supported planning claim: IIHR and IIVR list whitefly among major tomato pests; UAS Bengaluru identifies tomato plant-health and diagnostic capacity.
- Confusions: aphids, thrips, leaf-curl disease, nutrient stress, and general leaf curling.
- Required questions: Are small insects visible on leaf undersides? Are young leaves curling? Is there sticky residue? Are multiple plants affected?
- Safe preliminary advice: inspect several plants and record insect location and damage pattern; do not provide a pesticide dose.
- Refer when: vector-like symptoms are widespread or chemical control is being considered.

#### `tomato-fruit-borer-001` — partial evidence

- Supported planning claim: ICAR-IIVR and IIHR list fruit borer as a major tomato pest; the inspected pages do not justify a universal treatment schedule.
- Confusions: mechanical injury, fruit rot, cracking, and other boring pests.
- Required questions: Are there entry holes? Is frass present? Which fruit stage is affected? What proportion of fruit is damaged?
- Safe preliminary advice: inspect affected fruit and document the damage; do not recommend chemical treatment without current local advice.
- Refer when: damage is spreading or marketable fruit loss is significant.

#### `tomato-leaf-miner-001` — partial evidence

- Supported planning claim: ICAR-IIVR and IIHR list leaf miner/serpentine leaf miner among tomato pests.
- Confusions: leaf spots, nutrient injury, and physical damage.
- Required questions: Are there winding mines inside leaves? Are mines present on several plants? What crop stage is affected?
- Safe preliminary advice: photograph the mine pattern and inspect multiple plants before identifying the pest.
- Refer when: damage is increasing or a control decision is required.

### Fruit-related disorders

#### `tomato-fruit-cracking-001` — insufficient evidence

- The inspected sources mention fruit traits and production, but no sufficiently specific, Karnataka-relevant fruit-cracking diagnosis guidance was verified.
- Research required: direct IIHR/IIVR or UAS extension material describing distinguishing symptoms, environmental context, and safe non-chemical steps.

#### `tomato-blossom-end-rot-001` — insufficient evidence

- No inspected source in this review directly supported a safe tomato-specific record for blossom-end rot.
- Research required: a direct authoritative source covering symptoms, look-alikes, irrigation/nutrient context, and limits on advice.

## Source-quality decisions

1. The IIHR tomato portal is appropriate for planning and direct narrow claims because its tomato subpages expose crop-production and pest/disease-management content. A general IIHR landing page alone would not be treated as evidence for a specific symptom.
2. The IIVR page is strong for the existence of named cultivation, disease, and pest categories. It is not enough by itself to populate individual symptom-diagnosis records.
3. UAS Bengaluru pages are appropriate for Karnataka referral pathways and institutional diagnostic capability. They are not substitutes for a disease-specific extension bulletin.
4. The IIHR production page contains fertilizer and chemical-control details. Those should not be copied into this assistant as universal recommendations; this plan deliberately excludes doses.
5. Kannada translations should remain absent or explicitly pending until a qualified, source-checked translation review is completed.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Waterlogging/drainage and water-stress triage: highest immediate farmer usefulness and the strongest directly inspected support.
2. Pest observation records for whitefly, fruit borer, and leaf miner: the institutions list these pests, and the engine can safely ask observation questions without prescribing chemicals.
3. Bacterial wilt and leaf-curl referral records: high consequence, but keep the output explicitly non-diagnostic and referral-oriented.
4. Early and late blight records after direct symptom-level source review.
5. Nutrient deficiency records only after soil/tissue-test context and source-backed symptom differentiation are available.
6. Fruit cracking and blossom-end rot after direct authoritative source retrieval.
7. Fusarium wilt remains research backlog until a direct authoritative tomato-specific source is inspected.

## Source-access limitations

- Some official pages expose summary claims but not a publication/update date.
- The IIHR portal contains linked subpages and image-driven management sections; some linked image resources were not fetchable through the research tool.
- The inspected sources do not provide verified Kannada translations for the proposed records.
- The research tool surfaced official pages, but this review did not contact an agricultural expert or validate claims through a field diagnosis.
